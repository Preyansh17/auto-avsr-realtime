**Week:** 2026-06-23 → 2026-07-01
**Branch:** `realtime-port`
**Focus:** Diagnose why patient WER is high, fix real bugs, pin down the reproducible best recipe — then build audio-only ASR baselines and discover the "best recipe" itself has ~24pp unseeded run-to-run variance.

## TL;DR

- Early in the week: the **proven best recipe was 41.7%** streaming WER (legal, mouth-ROI, LoRA). Most of the week's first half was ruling out plausible-but-wrong improvements (face crops, val-loss/val-WER checkpoint selection, LR annealing, bf16) — all **neutral or harmful** — plus real bug fixes (audio-only wiring, mediapipe_face crop, bf16 loss crash).
- Built three audio-only ASR baseline pipelines (SpecAugment ablation harness, Whisper finetune, Nemotron streaming finetune) per the task-design doc, as standalone code — not yet run (HPC envs pending).
- Ported SpecAugment (Green-style: cut freq masking, blown-up time masking) into the **current AV Emformer**, gated behind `--specaug`/`SPECAUG=1`.
- A SpecAugment-vs-baseline comparison gave a suspicious result (46% vs 66%, vs the known 42%). Investigating it overturned the week's own headline number: this recipe has **~24pp run-to-run WER variance when unseeded** — the 41.7% was a lucky draw, not the expected result.
- Added `--seed`/`SEED` (reproducible runs — works) and `--avg-last-n` (average last-N epoch checkpoints instead of last-epoch-only — tested, **does not help**, made variance worse).
- Real baseline (median over 3 seeds, 2850 steps): **50.5% WER**.
- SpecAugment-vs-baseline, matched seeds: a **tie at 2850 steps** (0.505 vs 0.502) and **trends worse at 5700 steps** (0.612 vs 0.685) — no evidence it helps this recipe either way.
- Also seed-verified the "5700 steps overfits AV" claim (previously a single unseeded run): **holds up** — 5700 is 10.7pp worse than 2850, median-to-median, same seeds. Audio-only@5700 (median 46.6%) roughly confirms the previously-untrusted 45.2% single-run number.
- Got the Whisper pipeline actually running (two real bugs fixed: missing `sentencepiece`, gradient-checkpointing/freeze conflict). Whisper-small under Green's freeze-first-5 recipe: **29.2% median** — already beats the AV recipe outright. SpecAugment tested again here: tie, same null result as AV.
- Tested whether Green's freeze actually helps Whisper (it was designed for a different paper's model): **it doesn't — full finetune beats it, on every model size tried.** Small full-FT: 23.8%. Medium full-FT: 18.4%. SpecAugment gave a real, non-overlapping-range win on medium full-FT (−6.5pp → 11.9%) and replicated on turbo full-FT (−2.7pp → 12.4%, though turbo didn't beat medium — its distilled 4-layer decoder trades away capacity this task needs). Then tested full whisper-large-v3 (32-layer decoder, confirming the decoder-capacity theory): 8.1% median WER, believed at the time to be the best result of the week by a wide margin — and here SpecAugment REVERSES, hurting rather than helping (+2.7pp). **Correction (§28-29): this 8.1% turned out to be an artifact of a checkpoint-selection bug (best-of-30-epochs selected AND reported on the same val set) — under an honest three-way train/select/test split, large-v3 (no SpecAugment) scores 14.2% and medium+specaug (9.8%) briefly looked like the best real config.**
- **Second correction (§30): that too was incomplete** — large-v3 + SpecAugment had never been tested under the honest split (only its no-SpecAugment sibling had). Ran it: **5.46% legal_only / 2.64% merged — the actual best Whisper result of the whole week**, beating medium+SpecAugment. SpecAugment's effect on large-v3 **reverses a second time**: appeared to hurt under the old biased methodology (+2.7pp), turns out to help enormously once the bias is removed (−8.7pp). A follow-up epoch-count sweep (15/30/60) on this new best config confirmed 30 epochs is a genuine non-monotonic sweet spot, same shape as the AV Emformer's own step sweep — no further gain found by tuning it.
- **AV Emformer never had proper checkpoint selection either (§31)** — merge-last was the only thing ever tried, and the two "smart" selection metrics investigated earlier in the week (val_loss, greedy-utterance val_wer) were both shown broken. Built real streaming-mode selection (decode every candidate epoch in actual chunked streaming mode, pick the best on a selection set, report on a disjoint held-out set) — the gap versus merge-last is enormous and consistent across all 6 seeds tested (17-42pp). New honest numbers: **full-AV 36.6%** (was 50.3%), **audio-only 26.8%** (was 45.4%) — audio-only now sits at #4 overall, ahead of Nemotron.

## What we did

### 1. Audio-only modality — fixed (108.9% → 45.2%)
Audio-only inference was feeding the raw 512-d audio frontend straight into the Emformer, bypassing the pretrained fusion module. The Emformer expects fusion-space input, so the encoder output was out-of-distribution and LoRA could not remap it — the decoder free-ran on its language prior (108.9% WER, transcripts decoupled from input, val_loss stuck ~38).
**Fix (`f177964`):** keep the pretrained fusion and zero-fill the absent stream, so the Emformer always sees in-distribution features. Audio-only now trains (45.2%).

### 2. Face crops — bug found, fixed, re-run, and closed as a dead end
The face-crop experiment (convert mouth-ROI patient data to full-face crops matching the pretrained pipeline) scored 59–60% and even below audio-only. Investigation:
- **Detector bug (`bea5189`):** `mediapipe_face` returned its second alignment point as the raw bbox `(width, height)` instead of the bottom-right coordinate `(xmin+width, ymin+height)`. The affine warp cropped a random background region — the "face" crops contained a window, not a face. Coverage reported "0 failed" because the crop step never errors. The earlier "precomputed-face ≡ on-the-fly face" check was circular (both used the broken detector).
- After fixing the detector and regenerating the data (crops verified to be real faces), retraining and re-evaluating gave **60.2%** — still ~18pp worse than mouth-ROI (41.7%).
- **Conclusion:** at the model's 44×44 input, a full face puts the mouth in ~12 px vs ~44 px for a mouth ROI. The weak Linear video frontend can't use it, so face video becomes noise. Face crops are a genuine dead end; keep mouth ROIs.

### 3. bf16 support — crash fixed (`1e04345`)
`bf16-mixed` crashed at the RNN-T loss (`RuntimeError: logits must be float32 or float16`). torchaudio's `rnnt_loss` kernel rejects bfloat16.
**Fix:** cast the joiner logits to fp32 before the loss (no-op for fp32, safer for fp16, unblocks bf16). Later found bf16 is ~5pp worse than fp16 for WER, so the fix stays as a safety net but fp16 remains the default.

### 4. Checkpoint selection — val_loss (and val_wer) mislead
Found that **val_loss anti-correlates with WER** on these sets: the lowest-val_loss epoch (just past warmup) is undertrained for emission and decodes empty strings on ~half the clips (RNN-T blank over-emission), while later high-val_loss epochs stream better. Verified: merge-best-by-val_loss gave 77.7% (ROI) and 64.7–88.9% (face) vs merge-last ~42%/~60%. A merge-from-best commit was therefore **reverted** (`88882a0`).

### 5. LR schedule + WER-based selection — attempted, backfired, reverted
The cosine LR never annealed (`total_epochs` came from `--epochs=10000`, so over a 2850–5700-step run the LR stayed ~constant and val_loss "diverged" 15→60). Fixing it looked correct, so we shipped LR-annealing + per-epoch greedy-utterance val_wer + early-stop + merge-best (`6dc81a4`).
**Result: it regressed streaming WER 41.7% → ~60% across every checkpoint.** The constant-high-LR "divergent" schedule is actually load-bearing — it trains the model hard enough to decode in streaming mode (harder than utterance, less future context); the low-LR tail of an annealed run never gets there. Greedy-utterance val_wer (0.43) did not track streaming-beam WER (~60%) either.
**Reverted (`dffe398`):** `--anneal-lr` and `--select-by-wer` are now opt-in; defaults restore the proven recipe (constant LR, monitor val_loss, merge-last). val_wer is still logged for visibility. An isolation run confirmed removing the anneal recovers 60% → 47% (the rest of the 47→41.7 gap was, at the time, assumed to be bf16 vs fp16 — see §7 below for the real explanation).

### 6. Consistency fix (`d63032f`)
`dffe398` had swept in a half-committed SpecAugment feature (import in `module.py`, `--specaug` in `train.py`) without the new `specaug.py` or the data-module/transforms threading, breaking a fresh checkout (`ModuleNotFoundError`). Committed the rest of the feature; all backward-compatible (off unless `--specaug`).

### 7. Research — pretrained model provenance
Confirmed from the torchaudio recipe + Auto-AVSR paper + blog that the pretrained `device_avsr` model was trained **audio-visual with no modality dropout** (separate ASR/VSR/AV-ASR checkpoints exist upstream; we bootstrap the AV one). This explains why a zeroed stream is off-distribution and why audio-only (45.2%) trails true AV. Only the AV streaming checkpoint is published; there is no downloadable streaming audio-only or video-only model.

### 8. Audio ASR baselines (`asr_baselines/`) — SpecAugment, Whisper, Nemotron
Per the task-design doc (Green et al. 2021 dysarthric-ASR recipe: freeze all but the first ~5 encoder layers, SpecAugment with reduced frequency masking + massively increased time masking), built three standalone pipelines that reuse the existing patient label CSVs for `(wav, text)`:
- **Phase 1 — SpecAugment module** (`specaugment.py`, `visualize_specaug.py`, `configs/specaug_green.yaml`): reusable freq+time masking on log-mel, Green-tuned defaults (1 freq mask width 13, 10 time masks width 40, cap 0.5). Verified locally: masking shape correct, eval no-op, figure shows the expected heavy-time/light-freq pattern.
- **Phase 2 — Whisper** (`whisper_finetune.py`, `whisper_eval.py`): HF `Seq2SeqTrainer`, freeze-first-5-encoder-layers via `--unfreeze-encoder-layers`, built-in SpecAugment (`--no-specaug` for the ablation), checkpoint selection on WER not loss.
- **Phase 3 — Nemotron streaming** (`make_nemo_manifest.py`, `nemotron_finetune.py`, `nemotron_eval.py`): FastConformer-CacheAware-RNNT (`nvidia/nemotron-speech-streaming-en-0.6b`), same freeze + SpecAugment recipe via NeMo's native config, WER + RTF eval.
- Shared: `patient_audio.py` ((wav,text) from the CSVs, decoded from canonical SentencePiece tokens so WER is comparable across all models), `metrics.py` (one normalizer + WER/CER, `jiwer` or builtin Levenshtein fallback).
- SLURM templates (`slurm/whisper_finetune.sbatch`, `slurm/nemotron_finetune.sbatch`) + per-phase `requirements-*.txt` (separate conda envs — NeMo's torch pin clashes with both the AV env and the Whisper env).

**Status: code complete, committed, not yet run.** Needs `whisper_asr` and `nemo_asr` conda envs built on HPC (ran into an inode-quota issue building `whisper_asr`; `~/.cache` had to be cleared to free inodes) and models pre-cached from a login node before submitting.

### 9. SpecAugment ported to the current AV Emformer
Brought SpecAugment to the model actually in use (not just the new Whisper/Nemotron baselines). The audio frontend here is raw-waveform (`Conv1dResNet`, no mel spectrogram), so classic frequency-bin masking has no axis — implemented two complementary pieces, both gated behind one flag:
- **A — heavier waveform/video time-masking** (`transforms.py`): Green-style wider/denser `AdaptiveTimeMask` on the raw audio waveform and video frames (train split only).
- **B — feature-space SpecAugment** (`specaug.py`, new `FeatureSpecAugment`): time masking + channel masking (the no-mel analog of frequency masking) on the fused `(B,T,C)` features, applied in the training step only.
- Wired through `--specaug` / `SPECAUG=1` (both `train.py` and the LoRA/finetune sbatch templates), recorded in the run manifest. Zero new learnable parameters, so it doesn't affect checkpoint loading; off by default is byte-identical to prior behavior (verified via `_step` smoke test on CPU).

### 10. WER discrepancy investigation → unseeded run-to-run variance (overturns §5's assumption)
First real SpecAugment-vs-baseline ablation run: SpecAugment 46% WER vs a same-day baseline of 66% — both far off the previously-recorded 41.7%. Root-caused by:
1. Re-ran `eval.py` on the **original 41.7% checkpoint** with the current code: got **bit-identical WER** (0.41736772486772483, matching June to 16 digits). This proves eval.py and the current code are not the cause — the difference lives entirely in training.
2. Diffed run manifests (precision, steps, LR, LoRA config, data root, SentencePiece hash) between the 66% run and the 41.7% run: **identical** in every recorded field.
3. Diffed training logs: both runs reach the same epoch count, same trainable-param count, similar checkpoint-ladder val_losses, and both **diverge to val_loss ~27–29** by the final (merged) epoch — the recipe's constant-high-LR schedule is deliberately non-converging, and the merge step always takes the *last* epoch regardless.
4. Ran **3 fresh, unseeded, otherwise-identical baseline training runs**: WERs came back **0.574, 0.527, 0.618** — confirming the spread is inherent to the recipe, not a fluke of one bad run. Full population of identical-config runs observed this week: **0.417, 0.421, 0.470, 0.527, 0.574, 0.618, 0.663** (~24pp spread).

**Conclusion:** the divergent last-epoch endpoint is a high-variance region — tiny RNG differences flip individual clips between clean decode and empty (blank) output. The 41.7% quoted all week is a lucky tail of this distribution, not the expected result. (This also retroactively explains part of §5's "47 vs 41.7 gap" — likely variance, not purely bf16 vs fp16.)

### 11. Fix attempt 1 — seeding (works)
Added `--seed` / `SEED` env → `pytorch_lightning.seed_everything(seed, workers=True)`, recorded in the manifest, threaded through the LoRA sbatch. Ran **3 seeded baseline runs** (seed 1/2/3, otherwise identical config): **0.580, 0.505, 0.504 → median 0.505**. Each seed is now individually reproducible (re-running `SEED=1` reproduces its exact WER). Residual ~8pp seed-to-seed spread remains — smaller than the ~24pp unseeded spread, but not eliminated; it's intrinsic to the divergent-endpoint recipe, not removable by seeding alone.

### 12. Fix attempt 2 — last-N checkpoint averaging (does not help)
Hypothesis: average the last N epoch checkpoints (SWA-style) instead of merging only the single last epoch, to smooth out the endpoint lottery. Implemented `--avg-last-n` (`train.py`, `online_avsr/module.py`): a second `ModelCheckpoint` keeps the last N *consecutive* epochs (ranked by a newly-logged `epoch_idx`, since the default top-k-by-val_loss checkpoints are the wrong — early, undertrained — set to average), then averages them for the LoRA merge via the existing `average_checkpoints` helper.

Tested avg-last-5 on the same 3 seeds: **0.670, 0.536, 0.478 → median 0.536, spread 19pp** (vs 0.505 median / 7.6pp spread without averaging). **Result: worse median, wider spread.** Averaging only denoises when checkpoints share a flat basin; this recipe's constant-high-LR tail is chaotic (val_loss actively diverging), so the averaged epochs are incoherent and merging them degrades rather than smooths. `--avg-last-n` stays in the code (useful primitive) but defaults to **off (0)** — last-epoch-only remains the recipe.

### 14. Config verification caught a real mismatch — max_steps
Before trusting any new comparison, checked whether the untrusted single-run audio-only number (45.2%, job `lora_all_audio_legal_1782340900`) used the same config as the AV baseline convention. It didn't: that run used **`--max-steps 5700`**, not 2850. ### 27. WER metric bug — AV's macro-average was never comparable to Whisper/Nemotron's pooled WER
Code-review finding (later verified, not assumed): `eval.py`'s summary WER (`avg_wer`) is `statistics.mean(per_clip_wer)` — a **macro average** of per-clip ratios, every clip weighted equally regardless of length. `asr_baselines/metrics.py` (used for all Whisper/Nemotron numbers) instead calls `jiwer.wer(refs, hyps)`, which computes the standard **pooled/corpus WER** (total edit distance ÷ total reference words across the whole set) — the convention used by jiwer, Kaldi's `compute-wer`, NIST SCTK, and virtually every published ASR paper. These are different quantities: with short clips (this dataset averages ~5 words/clip) and the Emformer's sentence-initial-word-drop failure mode, a single dropped word on a short clip swings that clip's ratio by 0.20, and macro-averaging lets that dominate — every AV row in the table above was silently on a different metric than every Whisper/Nemotron row.

Also found, same review: `online_avsr/text.py`'s `normalize_text` kept apostrophes (`"don't"` → 1 token) while `asr_baselines/metrics.py`'s `normalize` stripped them (`"don't"` → `"don t"`, 2 tokens) — a second source of non-comparability. Empirically this made zero difference on the actual data (no contractions in these transcripts), but it was a real inconsistency, not just theoretical.

**Fixes:**
- `online_avsr/text.py`: apostrophe now stripped, matching `metrics.py` exactly.
- `eval.py`: summary now reports both `avg_wer` (macro, kept for back-compat/diagnostics) and a new `corpus_wer` (pooled — the one to actually table-compare against Whisper/Nemotron going forward).

**Retroactive audit:** recomputed pooled WER for every AV configuration in the Reference Results table above, directly from each run's saved `results.jsonl` transcripts (no retraining/re-eval needed — the raw ref/hyp text was already on disk). Pooled WER is consistently **1–3pp lower** than the old macro numbers, same direction across every single run — **no conclusion in this document changes** (seeded-baseline-2850 remains the best real AV number at 50.3% pooled vs 50.5% macro; 5700/avg-last-5/SpecAugment orderings are all unchanged), but the exact headline numbers do, and any future AV-vs-Whisper-vs-Nemotron comparison should use `corpus_wer`, not `avg_wer`.

### 28. Whisper checkpoint-selection bug — best-of-30 selected AND reported on the same val set
Second code-review finding, also verified rather than assumed: `whisper_finetune.py:240-241` sets `load_best_model_at_end=True, metric_for_best_model="wer"` — the checkpoint kept at the end of training is whichever of the ~30 epochs scored best WER on `--val-file`. `whisper_finetune.sbatch:97` then runs the final reported eval on that **same** `VAL_FILE`. This is best-of-N selection and reporting on identical data — a real optimistic bias. Neither the AV Emformer (`train.py`, merge-last, no selection) nor Nemotron (`nemotron_finetune.py`, `model.save_to()` called unconditionally after `trainer.fit()`, no selection) do this — both report whatever the literal last epoch produced. The comparison tables above were asymmetric in Whisper's favor.

**Quantified, not estimated:** `Seq2SeqTrainingArguments(save_total_limit=3)` only keeps the 3 most-recent epoch checkpoints plus the selected-best one, but for a 30-epoch run that means the true final epoch (epoch 30) is still on disk (within the last 3 saved) for every run checked. Evaluated the actual epoch-30 checkpoint directly — no retraining needed — for the two configs that matter most (current "best overall" and current headline):

| config | best-of-30 (as tabulated above) | **true last-epoch** | gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Whisper-medium full-FT + SpecAugment, seed 1/2/3 | 11.9% / 14.6% / 11.4% | 16.2% / 16.2% / 17.3% | |
| **median** | **11.9%** | **16.2%** | **+4.3pp** |
| Whisper-large-v3 full-FT, no SpecAug, seed 1/2/3 | 8.1% / 8.1% / 11.9% | 9.7% / 13.0% / 17.3% | |
| **median** | **8.1%** | **13.0%** | **+4.9pp** |

The selection bias is real and substantial (~4-5pp optimistic on both configs), not a rounding-level effect. Whisper still wins decisively over AV (50.3%) and Nemotron (35.7%) either way — the headline conclusion "Whisper >> AV/Nemotron" survives — but the margin is smaller than tabulated, and best-of-30 numbers should not be read as apples-to-apples against AV/Nemotron's last-epoch numbers.

**Not fixed in code yet** (unlike §27's metric bug, this is a methodology/protocol choice, not a pure bug) — options, per the review that surfaced this:
1. Three-way split (separate selection-val and reporting-test sets) — most correct, but this dataset is tiny (268 train / 30 val for legal_only); splitting the 30-clip val set further would make both the selection signal and the reporting number noisier.
2. Report last-epoch Whisper alongside best-of-30 for every config (what this section did for the two most important configs) — cheapest, no retraining, but doesn't scale to redoing it for all ~20 Whisper configs in this document without more compute time.
3. Select best-checkpoint on a held-out subset disjoint from the final reporting set.
Flagged as open work — acted on immediately after for the two configs that matter most (§29).

### 31. AV Emformer — real streaming-mode checkpoint selection, a massive and consistent win
The AV Emformer has always used merge-last (no selection at all). Nobody had tried selecting on the metric that actually matters — real chunked streaming decode — because it's expensive (full windowed inference per candidate checkpoint, not a cheap training-loop side effect like the other two).

**Built it anyway.** `train.py`'s existing `--avg-last-n` mechanism (originally built for checkpoint averaging, §12 last week — that averaging itself was found to hurt) already keeps the last N consecutive epoch LoRA checkpoints on disk as a side effect (`recent-epoch=N.ckpt`). ## Key lessons

1. **The streaming-beam eval is the only trustworthy metric.** val_loss and greedy-utterance val_wer both mislead (blank-emission dynamics).
2. **"Broken-looking" training was accidentally optimal** — up to a point. Constant high LR + long training + merge-last beats the principled-looking annealed/early-stopped/best-selected alternative — but it also means the "best" number is a lottery draw, not a fixed point.
3. **This recipe has ~24pp unseeded WER variance.** Any single-run comparison (including the previously-published 41.7%) is not meaningful on its own — it could be any point in a wide, mostly-unlucky distribution.
4. **Verify data, not just that a job ran.** The face-crop coverage said "0 failed" while cropping windows; only looking at a frame caught it. Likewise, bit-identical re-eval of an old checkpoint is the right first check when a number looks wrong.
5. **Not every "smooth it out" idea works.** Checkpoint averaging is a reasonable-sounding fix that actively hurts here because the averaged region is chaotic, not just noisy — always verify a proposed fix rather than assuming it helps.
6. **Seeding is necessary but not sufficient.** It makes results reproducible, but doesn't shrink the recipe's intrinsic variance (~8pp remains even seeded) — real comparisons need multiple seeds per condition and a median/spread, not a single run.
7. High WER here is partly a resolution/data-scale ceiling (268 clips, 44×44, AV-only pretraining) and partly this endpoint-lottery variance — not a simple config bug.

Likely cause: `--unfreeze-encoder-layers 5` freezes the same absolute layer count regardless of depth, so it leaves only **8.2% of medium trainable** (62.98M/763.86M) vs **14.7% of small** (35.44M/241.73M, freeze-5-of-12 vs freeze-5-of-24). Green's "first 5 layers" was calibrated for a specific (shallower) model; applied literally to a deeper encoder it under-trains medium relative to its capacity. Combined with the never-tuned `--epochs 30` (§17), this comparison isn't yet apples-to-apples on trainable-fraction or training-length grounds — before concluding medium is genuinely worse, would need to scale the freeze depth (or unfreeze a comparable *fraction* of layers) and re-sweep epochs for medium specifically.

### 20. Whisper-small, full finetune (no freeze at all) — beats the Green-recipe freeze
Given §19's freeze-depth confound, tested the other end directly: does Green's freeze (designed to prevent overfitting on tiny dysarthric datasets) help at all on Whisper? Ran 3 seeded whisper-small runs with `--unfreeze-encoder-layers -1 --train-decoder --no-specaug` (every param trainable, encoder + decoder + conv stem, no SpecAugment), same seeds/data as §17/§18. Confirmed via the log that `apply_freeze()` correctly no-ops for this config (no "Freeze:" line printed at all — the intended early-return path, not a bug).

There was a real prior against this working: the AV Emformer's full-finetune on the same 268 clips **collapsed** (results §1 in the earlier week: 18/30 empty transcripts, ~10pp worse than LoRA) — exactly the overfitting Green's freeze exists to prevent.

| seed | WER |
|---|---|
| 1 | 0.2486 |
| 2 | 0.2162 |
| 3 | 0.2378 |
| **median** | **0.2378** |

**No collapse — full finetune is BETTER than the frozen recipe**, not worse: 23.8% median vs 28.7–29.2% for freeze-first-5. Tight spread too (~3.2pp). This is the opposite outcome from the AV Emformer at the same data scale. Likely explanation: Whisper-small's pretraining is broad/strong enough (large-scale general ASR) that it doesn't need the overfitting guardrail Green's freeze provides — that guardrail seems to matter more for architectures/pretraining regimes closer to the AV Emformer's (smaller pretrain corpus, streaming-specific). **The Green freeze recipe, imported wholesale from a different paper/model, is not obviously the right choice here** — full finetune is currently the best Whisper-small result found this session.

### 21. Full finetune + SpecAugment — still an exact tie
All prior SpecAugment comparisons (§13 AV, §18 Whisper) were run under a FROZEN config (only 14.7% trainable), where SpecAugment's anti-overfitting regularization has less to protect against. Full finetune (§20, 100% trainable) is the regime where regularization should matter most if it matters at all — and had never been tested with SpecAugment on. Ran 3 seeded full-finetune runs with `SPECAUG=1` (otherwise identical to §20):

| seed | with SpecAug | without SpecAug (§20) | Δ |

**Exact tie on the median**, per-seed deltas still flip sign. Combined with §13 (AV, frozen) and §18 (Whisper, frozen): four config combinations tied so far. (§23 below finds the exception: it stops being a tie once the model is BOTH large AND fully unfrozen.)

### 22. Whisper-medium, full finetune — new best result, and "bigger model helps" holds once unfrozen
§19 showed medium underperforming small under the Green freeze, attributed to the fixed-layer-count freeze starving medium's capacity (8.2% trainable vs small's 14.7%). §20 showed full finetune beats freezing on small. This tests the natural next question: does full finetune let medium's extra capacity actually help, the way "bigger pretrained model" naively should? Ran 3 seeded whisper-medium runs with `--unfreeze-encoder-layers -1 --train-decoder --no-specaug`, `--batch-size 4 --grad-accum 4` (halved from the default 8/2 as a memory precaution for full backward through 769M params — confirmed no OOM, could likely go back to 8 if needed later).

| seed | WER |
|---|---|
| 1 | 0.1568 |
| 2 | 0.1946 |
| 3 | 0.1838 |
| **median** | **0.1838** |

**New best result by a wide margin — 18.4% median**, beating small's full-FT (23.8%) by 5.4pp. Training logs showed train loss underflowing to `0.0` by ~epoch 18–20 (near-total memorization of the 268 training clips in bf16 precision) — not a bug, and not fatal: `load_best_model_at_end=True` + `metric_for_best_model=wer` means the saved checkpoint is whichever epoch had the best held-out WER, not the last, so late-stage memorization past that point doesn't corrupt the final result.

The pattern is now consistent: on this data, **full finetune + bigger pretrained model wins**, and Green's freeze-first-5-layers recipe (built for a different paper's dysarthric-speech setup) is actively hurting Whisper here, not helping — the opposite of its intended effect, and the opposite of the AV Emformer's behavior at the same data scale (where full finetune collapsed).

### 23. Whisper-medium full-FT + SpecAugment — first genuine, non-noise win

Medium full-FT (§22, the current best config, 100% of the largest tested model trainable) is the highest-overfitting-risk setting tried so far — theoretically where SpecAugment's regularization should matter most. **Real effect, not noise: every with-SpecAugment seed (0.1135–0.1459) beats every without-SpecAugment seed (0.1568–0.1946) — the ranges don't overlap.** First genuine SpecAugment win across the whole investigation, after four ties. Consistent with the regularization story: it only shows up where there's real overfitting risk to guard against (full-finetune of the largest model on 268 clips), not under the frozen configs (where most params can't overfit anyway) or on the smaller model (less capacity to overfit with in the first place).

### 24. Whisper-large-v3-turbo full finetune — SpecAugment win replicates, but turbo doesn't beat medium
Tested whether Green's freeze is worth trying at all one size up (large family): no — the freeze-vs-full-FT gap widened monotonically small→medium (5pp→19pp) as freeze starves a shrinking fraction of a deeper encoder, so freeze was skipped entirely for large. Went straight to full finetune, and picked **whisper-large-v3-turbo** (808.9M params, 32 encoder / 4 decoder layers — v3's encoder with a distilled 4-layer decoder) over the full v3 decoder (32 layers) specifically to keep full-FT backward cost down, given medium already needed a reduced batch size. Ran the full 2×2 (3 seeds × with/without SpecAugment), `batch_size=4 grad_accum=4` (same as medium, no OOM). Model pre-caching needed `torch_dtype=bfloat16, low_cpu_mem_usage=True` on the login node after two plain loads got SIGKILL'd (memory pressure, not a hard per-process cap — `free -h` showed 80GB available at the time).

| seed | with SpecAug | without SpecAug | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.1243 | 0.1405 | −1.6pp |
| 2 | 0.1189 | 0.1514 | −3.3pp |
| 3 | 0.1405 | 0.1514 | −1.1pp |
| **median** | **0.1243** | **0.1514** | **−2.7pp** |

**SpecAugment's win replicates on a second model** — all 3 seeds move the same direction again, ranges essentially non-overlapping (with-SpecAugment max 0.1405 == without-SpecAugment min 0.1405). Two independent large-full-FT models now show a real, consistent SpecAugment benefit (medium: −6.5pp; turbo: −2.7pp) — this is no longer a single-model fluke.

**But turbo does not beat medium**: 0.1243 (turbo+SpecAugment) vs 0.1189 (medium+SpecAugment) — the ranges overlap heavily (turbo 0.1189–0.1405, medium 0.1135–0.1459), essentially a tie, not a new best. The "bigger model wins" pattern that held small→medium (23.8%→18.4%) does not clearly continue to turbo, despite turbo's encoder being deeper (32 vs 24 layers). Likely explanation: turbo trades depth for a much lighter decoder (4 layers, distilled, vs medium's full decoder) — for this task (short, closed-vocabulary sentences), decoder capacity for generation may matter as much as encoder depth for acoustic representation, and turbo gave up decoder capacity to get its encoder/speed advantage.

**Current best remains whisper-medium full-FT + SpecAugment at 11.9% median** (§23) — turbo was worth testing (confirms the SpecAugment finding generalizes, cheaper/faster to run than full large-v3 would have been) but doesn't unseat it. (§25 below tests full large-v3 and finds the decoder-capacity hypothesis correct — and overturns the "SpecAugment always helps large full-FT" read.)

### 25. Whisper-large-v3 full finetune — new best by a wide margin, and SpecAugment's pattern gets more nuanced
Directly tests §24's open question: does the FULL 32-layer decoder (not turbo's distilled 4-layer one) unlock more than turbo did? Pre-caching `openai/whisper-large-v3` (1543.5M params, 32/32 encoder/decoder layers) hit the same login-node SIGKILL as turbo even with `bf16 + low_cpu_mem_usage` (turbo's 809M/~1.6GB load succeeded that way, large-v3's 1550M/~3.1GB didn't) — pointed to a per-process memory cap on the login node specifically, not a real system OOM (`free -h` showed 80GB free at the time). Fixed by moving the pre-cache to a proper compute-node job with an explicit `--mem=16GB` allocation (CPU-only, no GPU needed just to download/cache) — succeeded immediately. Training used a more conservative `batch_size=2 grad_accum=8` (vs medium/turbo's 4/4) given the much heavier full decoder; no OOM.

| seed | with SpecAug | without SpecAug | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.1081 | 0.0811 | +2.7pp |
| 2 | 0.1135 | 0.0811 | +3.2pp |
| 3 | 0.1027 | 0.1189 | −1.6pp |
| **median** | **0.1081** | **0.0811** | **+2.7pp** |

2. ~~The decoder-capacity hypothesis from §24 is confirmed~~ — **superseded**: large-v3's advantage over medium/turbo does not survive an honest split; if anything the opposite (large-v3 generalizes worse than medium on this little data, likely overfitting given only ~238-268 training clips for a 1.5B-param full finetune).

## Open / next

**Current standing (as of §30-31, the latest corrections):**
- Best config overall: **Whisper large-v3 + SpecAugment, 5.46% (legal_only) / 2.64% (merged), honest three-way split** — see §30. This is the third headline number in a row for large-v3 (8.1% best-of-30 biased → 14.2% honest-no-specaug → 5.46% honest-with-specaug); treat any *future* large-v3 number with the same skepticism until it's been run under the full honest protocol.
- Best AV Emformer config: **audio-only, streaming-mode checkpoint selection, 26.8%** (§31) — not merge-last (43.7%). Full-AV (streaming-selected): 36.6%. Video still loses to audio-only within this architecture.
- Nemotron best: 33.3% (other file, §15).

**Open items:**
- **Small and turbo Whisper were never re-tested under the three-way split** — their reported numbers (23.8%, 15.1%/12.4%) carry the same unverified best-of-30 selection bias as everything except medium+specaug and large-v3 (both variants). Worth doing the same treatment before trusting the leaderboard ordering among small/turbo/medium/large-v3.
- 15-clip test sets are too noisy to trust for this kind of comparison (§29: same large-v3 config gave 27.9% and 4.44% on two different 15-clip splits) — any future eval-set-size decision for this data should default to the full 30-clip (or larger) set, not split it further.
- Whisper medium/turbo full-FT used `batch_size=4`, large-v3 used `batch_size=2` (both reduced from the default 8, as OOM precautions) — never confirmed whether higher batch sizes would actually OOM; could try reverting upward to speed up future runs if memory allows.
- **AV Emformer's streaming-selection sweep (§31) only covered the last 20 epochs** (`AVG_LAST_N=20`) — earlier epochs were never checked, on the assumption they're undertrained (consistent with prior findings), but that assumption itself hasn't been directly tested for this specific split.
- **AV Emformer was only tested on legal_only** — never run on merged (Whisper and Nemotron both were). Given how much merged helped Whisper (2-5x lower WER) and Nemotron (modestly), worth checking whether it helps the AV Emformer too.
- **AV Emformer full finetune (non-LoRA) has never been tried** — every AV Emformer number in this document, including the new §31 streaming-selected ones, is LoRA (r=8, alpha=16, scopes=all). Whether full finetune would close the gap to Whisper (the way it did for Whisper vs Green-freeze) is untested.
- **`slurm/select_best_streaming_epoch.sbatch` (§31) is a manual side-script, not integrated into the main training pipeline** — running it requires a separate invocation per training run and knowing to set `AVG_LAST_N` up front. If streaming-mode selection becomes the standard recipe going forward, it should probably get folded into `train_realtime_lora.sbatch` directly rather than staying a bolt-on.
- **Nemotron's own selection (other file, §15) selects on offline WER, not streaming WER** — the same class of metric-mismatch risk that turned out to matter enormously for the AV Emformer (§31: greedy/offline metrics don't track real streaming performance) hasn't been ruled out for Nemotron, which is also a streaming-first architecture. Worth checking whether Nemotron's offline-selected checkpoint is actually optimal under its own streaming eval, or whether streaming-based selection would help there too.
- Login-node pre-caching of large models (turbo, large-v3) repeatedly hit SIGKILL even with `bf16 + low_cpu_mem_usage` — moving pre-cache to a proper compute-node job with explicit `--mem=` is the reliable fix (confirmed for large-v3); worth doing this by default for any future model ≥1B params rather than debugging login-node memory limits again.
- 2850 steps is confirmed (seed-matched) as the better AV operating point vs 5700 for the OLD merge-last recipe — worth re-checking whether that still holds now that real streaming-selection is available (a different step count might interact differently with the selection sweep).
- Whisper-medium (37.3% median, Green freeze) underperformed small (29.2%) at the same `--unfreeze-encoder-layers 5` — that freezes a much smaller *fraction* of medium's deeper 24-layer encoder (8.2% trainable vs small's 14.7%). Re-test medium with a scaled freeze depth before concluding size doesn't help under Green freeze specifically (moot for the honest full-FT leaderboard, but still an open question about the freeze recipe itself).
- Attack the dominant residual error from earlier in the week: sentence-initial word drop (first word lost in Emformer segment-0, no left context) — still open, independent of the variance/selection findings.
- Consider a structural fix for the divergent-endpoint variance itself (e.g. a milder LR anneal that stops short of full convergence) rather than relying on post-hoc streaming-selection to route around it — selection works (§31) but a training-time fix would be cheaper than sweeping 20 epochs per run.
