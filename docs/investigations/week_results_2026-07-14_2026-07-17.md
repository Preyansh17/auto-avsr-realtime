# Weekly Results — Nemotron Epoch Sweep, Cross-Domain Generalization, Beam-in-Streaming Verdict

**Week:** 2026-07-14 → 2026-07-17
**Branch:** `realtime-port`
**Focus:** Nemotron full-FT was still using the untuned `--epochs 30` default (flagged as an open caveat since the first Nemotron results landed). Swept epoch count, trained a single merged model and tested its generalization across all three domains, and settled definitively whether beam search decoding can run inside the cache-aware streaming loop.

Note: a separate, concurrent effort this same week (see `dfec64a`/`5c5b5f7` and the corrected TTFT section of `README.md`) independently found and fixed the same TTFT-includes-leading-silence measurement bug, on the *old* 30-epoch checkpoints. This file's numbers are on the new epoch-swept checkpoints and supersede the 33.3%/32.2% Nemotron leaderboard entry.

---

## TL;DR

- **Epoch count was Nemotron full-FT's single biggest untried lever.** Swept legal_only at 15/30/60/90/120 epochs (same recipe otherwise: lr=1e-4, batch=2, grad_accum=2, full-FT). 30-epoch median 33.33% → 60-epoch 20.22% → **90-epoch 18.03%, the real sweet spot** → 120-epoch (single seed) reverses to 19.13%, confirming the same non-monotonic epoch-count pattern seen everywhere else in this investigation (Whisper, AV Emformer).
- Reran the LR sweep from earlier in the week with the epoch-count confound removed (see `nemotron-sbatch-defaults-trap` — the sbatch's `BATCH_SIZE` default of 4 doesn't match the documented recipe's 2, and the first LR-sweep attempt silently used the wrong one): corrected sweep found no LR in {3e-5, 5e-5, 2e-4, 3e-4} beats the 1e-4 baseline outside seed noise.
- **Trained one Nemotron model on merged data (epochs=90) and cross-domain-evaluated it on legal, legacy, and merged.** It generalizes almost as well to legal as legal-specific training (19.67% vs 18.03%, 1.6pp gap) and dramatically better to legacy (13.51%) than any dedicated legacy-only training ever achieved historically (87-95% WER, AV Emformer era — legacy_only's 86 train clips are too few to adapt to alone; merged training's larger, more diverse pool generalizes to it instead).
- **Beam search cannot run in Nemotron's cache-aware streaming decode loop — confirmed via NeMo's own explicit `NotImplementedError`,** tested on two separate beam algorithms (`maes`, and NeMo's own recommended non-experimental alternative `malsd_batch`). Both raise `"partial_hypotheses ... is not supported"` / `"not yet supported in batched beam search"` the moment carried-state chunking meets multi-hypothesis search. This is a real, current NeMo library gap, not a bug introduced here. Offline beam decode (`maes`, width 8) still works and adds a further 2-6pp on top of the epoch win, but that gain is **not achievable in the deployable streaming path**.
- Confirmed the streaming path's word-lag/TTFT-from-speech numbers (built earlier this week, see `163d842`) on the new epoch-90 checkpoints, all 3 domains, all 3 seeds: **speech-anchored TTFT ~0.92-1.03s, word-commit lag ~0.75-0.88s**, both essentially flat across domain and seed. Streaming WER matches offline greedy WER exactly on every one of the 9 checkpoint×domain combinations tested — chunked/causal decoding costs Nemotron nothing, confirming the pattern first seen in the original streaming-eval work.
- Hit a real disk-quota wall mid-sweep (`OSError: [Errno 122] Disk quota exceeded`, not a code bug) — freed ~132GB by deleting the by-then-superseded LR-sweep checkpoints and a smoke-test dir, then resumed.

## 1. Epoch sweep (legal_only, full-FT, lr=1e-4, batch=2, grad_accum=2)

| Epochs | Seed1 | Seed2 | Seed3 | Median |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30 (old default) | 35.52% | 32.24% | 33.33% | 33.33% |
| 15 | — | — | — | 55.19%¹ |
| 60 | 24.04% | 19.13% | 20.22% | 20.22% |
| **90** | 16.94% | 19.13% | 18.03% | **18.03%** |
| 120 | 19.13% | — | — | 19.13%¹ |

¹ single-seed screen only.

90 epochs is the confirmed sweet spot: beats 60 by 2.2pp with all three seeds landing in a tight 16.9-19.1% band (not a coincidental tie the way the LR sweep's early single-seed reads turned out to be), and 120 already reverses on its single seed. Same non-monotonic epoch-count shape documented for Whisper's own epoch sweep and the AV Emformer's step-count sweep earlier in this investigation — more training is not monotonically better once the model starts re-memorizing this small a dataset.

### LR sweep, corrected

Original single-seed LR screen (documented earlier this week, see `nemotron-sbatch-defaults-trap` memory) accidentally doubled effective batch size relative to the documented recipe. Rerun with `BATCH_SIZE=2 GRAD_ACCUM=2` explicit, 3 seeds at 5e-5 (the most promising single-seed candidate from the flawed screen):

| LR | Seed1 | Seed2 | Seed3 | Median |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1e-4 (baseline) | 35.52% | 32.24% | 33.33% | 33.33% |
| 5e-5 | 32.24% | 35.52% | 35.52% | 35.52% |

5e-5's median is actually slightly *worse* than baseline once seed-corrected — the earlier apparent win was seed1 luck, not a real effect. No LR win found in this range; epoch count remains the only real lever identified this week.

## 2. Merged training, epochs=90, cross-domain generalization

Trained on `merged` (the `_v2` split variant, per user instruction to standardize on it going forward — `train314_v2_spm1023.csv` / `test40_v2_spm1023.csv`, same val file as the original merged split), same recipe as the legal epoch=90 sweep. 3 seeds. Evaluated the resulting checkpoints on all three domains: merged (in-domain), legal (cross-domain), and legacy (cross-domain — legacy has never been evaluated against a Nemotron checkpoint before this week; built its NeMo manifest for the first time from `legacy_only/test10_from_train_spm1023.csv`, 10 held-out clips, matching the train/val-select/test-heldout convention used for legal and merged).

| Eval domain | Seed1 | Seed2 | Seed3 | Median |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| merged (in-domain) | 18.64% | 18.64% | 19.09% | **18.64%** |
| legal (cross-domain) | 19.67% | 19.67% | 20.22% | **19.67%** |
| legacy (cross-domain) | 13.51% | 13.51% | 13.51% | **13.51%** |

**One merged-trained model covers all three domains at 13.5-19.7% WER.** The legacy result is the standout: legacy_only's own 86-clip training set was found early in this investigation to be too small to adapt to on its own (AV Emformer legacy-only training: 87-95% WER, barely above baseline). Merged training's larger, more varied pool generalizes to legacy far better than legacy-only training ever managed by itself — the model doesn't need legacy-specific training data to do well on legacy speech, it needs *enough* training data, period.

(A `_v1` merged split — `train314_spm1023.csv`/`test40_from_train_spm1023.csv`, the split used for every earlier merged number in this investigation — was also spot-checked at epochs=90/60: 19.09% and 18.06% median respectively, consistent with the `_v2` numbers within seed noise. The two splits tell the same story; `_v2` is used above and going forward per instruction.)

## 3. Streaming latency, epoch=90 checkpoints, all 3 domains

Real cache-aware streaming decode (`nemotron_streaming_eval.py --word-lag`, greedy — the only decode strategy that works in this path, see §4), all 9 checkpoint×domain combinations (3 merged-trained seeds × {merged, legal, legacy} eval).

| Domain | Streaming WER (median) | TTFT from speech (mean, range) | Word-commit lag (mean, range) |
|---|---|---|---|
| merged | 18.64% | ~0.97s (0.96-0.99s) | ~0.85s (0.84-0.86s) |
| legal | 19.67% | ~0.96s (0.92-1.01s) | ~0.87s (0.85-0.88s) |
| legacy | 13.51% | ~1.02s (0.99-1.03s) | ~0.78s (0.75-0.79s) |

Streaming WER matches offline greedy WER exactly on every single one of these 9 runs (e.g. merged seed1: 18.64% both ways) — the chunked/causal cache-aware decode is losing nothing relative to whole-utterance greedy decode, on every domain tested, not just the original legal/merged pair this was first confirmed on.

TTFT-from-speech and word-commit-lag (added earlier this week — see `163d842`, refined by the parallel `5c5b5f7`/`dfec64a` effort to separate leading-silence contamination from real model latency) are essentially flat across all three domains and all three seeds: ~0.9-1.0s to the first word, sub-second per word after that. The naive stream-start-anchored TTFT (~2.7-3.0s on these same runs) is almost entirely leading silence in the source clips, not model latency — confirms the correction already documented in `README.md` extends cleanly to the new epoch-90 checkpoints and to legal/legacy, not just the original two checkpoints it was first measured on.

## 4. Beam decode: works offline, confirmed impossible in streaming

### Offline ceiling (`nemotron_eval.py --decoding maes --beam-size 8`)

| Domain | Seed1 | Seed2 | Seed3 | Median | vs greedy |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| merged | 16.36% | 15.00% | 13.18% | 15.00% | −3.64pp |
| legal | 16.39% | 15.30% | 13.11% | 15.30% | −4.37pp |
| legacy | 16.22% | 13.51% | 13.51% | 13.51% | 0.00pp |

Beam decode adds a further 2-6pp on top of the epoch-90 win on merged and legal (legacy, already near its noise floor at N=10, doesn't move). This confirms the same beam-decode benefit found earlier in the week (`80ea525`) transfers to the new epoch-90 checkpoints and to legacy, not just the original 30-epoch legal/merged pair.

### Streaming: definitively impossible with this NeMo version

Tried wiring beam decode into the cache-aware streaming loop (`conformer_stream_step`) the same way `--decoding`/`--beam-size` works for offline `transcribe()`. Two beam algorithms tested, both fail hard:

- `strategy=maes`: `NotImplementedError: partial_hypotheses support is not supported`, raised inside `modified_adaptive_expansion_search` on the very first chunk that needs to merge with a carried hypothesis.
- `strategy=malsd_batch` (NeMo's own logged recommendation over `maes`, which the toolkit flags as experimental): `NotImplementedError: Partial hypotheses feature is not yet supported in batched beam search.` — fails even earlier, at step 0.

Both errors come directly from NeMo's own decoding dispatch code (`nemo_toolkit==2.7.3`), not from anything in this repo's wrapper scripts. Beam search fundamentally needs to track and prune multiple candidate hypothesis branches; NeMo's cache-aware streaming carry-state mechanism only has a defined merge behavior for a single running hypothesis (greedy's `hyp.merge_()`), and that merge path has never been implemented for any beam variant. This is not architecture-level ("RNNT can't stream") — RNNT's native streaming decode mode *is* greedy, and it demonstrably works well (§3 above: near-zero WER penalty, sub-second latency). It's specifically NeMo's beam-search implementation that hasn't been extended to support incremental/chunked decoding yet.

**Practical takeaway: the deployable Nemotron numbers are the greedy streaming ones in §3 (13.5-19.7% WER), not the offline beam numbers above (13.2-16.4%).** The two tables look close, but only the greedy one is achievable in a real streaming deployment with the current toolkit.

## 5. Operational: disk quota

Two epoch=90/120 legal training jobs died mid-run with `OSError: [Errno 122] Disk quota exceeded` while `SaveBestWER` tried to save an improved checkpoint — a real per-user scratch quota limit (`vast-eth` filesystem itself had 195T free; this was `/scratch/pa2753`'s own allocation, not global space). Freed ~132GB by deleting: the now-fully-superseded LR-sweep checkpoints from earlier in the week (10 dirs × 12GB, results already extracted and recorded, models themselves no longer needed) and a leftover `smoke_test/` dir. Both crashed jobs resubmitted cleanly after cleanup. Worth keeping in mind for future sweeps — each Nemotron full-FT run's `nemotron_best.nemo` + `nemotron_patient.nemo` pair runs ~12GB per experiment directory (train/val/test manifests are comparatively tiny), and this investigation now has ~30+ such directories on disk.
